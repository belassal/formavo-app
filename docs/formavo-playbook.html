Formavo · Internal playbook

Know the product cold

The whole of Formavo in one read — the machine, the layers, who benefits how, and why it wins. Read before any pitch; the coach-facing one-pager is separate.

One coach logging a game from their phone feeds every report a parent, coach, or club administrator will ever ask for.

That's the thesis. Everyone else built admin software with the match as an afterthought. Formavo is built around the touchline — data is captured as a byproduct of coaching, so the reports are free.

The machine — one flywheel

  1. BeforeFixtures imported from the league's schedule link. Parents RSVP'd for their own kids. Lineup set — or loaded from a saved one.
  2. During — the Game Day consoleReal clock. Tap the scorer on the pitch to log a goal (assist + shot location). Drag subs; minutes track themselves through rolling subs. Switch formation mid-game. 🎙 voice notes — minute-stamped, staff-only, parents can never access them. Everything undoable.
  3. As it happensParents get the score pushed live — from work, from the car, from another kid's game.
  4. Full timeCloud functions build the summary and season aggregates. Nobody does paperwork. The recap — timeline, shot map, minutes — is shareable instantly, with the club's sponsor on it.
  5. Over the seasonThose aggregates power player profiles, season cards, leaderboards with league/cup/friendly filters, and every club report. The season writes itself.

Three layers on one spine

Team — the daily driver

Schedule with next-up hero, countdown, RSVP glance, weather · trainings with recurring series, RSVP and check-in · announcements, chat, match-tagged photos · and a roster where every player has season stats, a shareable Season Card, post-match ratings feeding a development log, and four-corner assessments (Technical / Tactical / Physical / Mental).

Parent — read-and-RSVP, by design

Live scores, schedule, their own kids' stats, chat — and structurally nothing else. Enforced in server-side security rules, not hidden buttons. Coach material (voice notes, ratings, assessments) is architecturally unreachable by parents. A real safeguarding story for clubs.

Club — where the money is

The club is the tenant. Staff carry real per-team positions from a club-managed catalog. Club Reports: per-competition records · playing-time equity with a club-set fairness threshold · position exposure · coach adoption · staff headcounts. Plus tryouts with shortlists, club identity, and a sponsor line sold on every recap.

Who benefits, and how

The coach gets time + credibility

Roster pasted from a spreadsheet once — parent invites go out automatically. Fixtures imported once. From then on their only job is coaching. When a parent challenges playing time, the coach has minutes per player. Discussing development, they have ratings, assessments, and their own in-the-moment voice notes instead of fuzzy memory.

The club gets visibility + governance

A president today has no idea what happens inside twelve teams. Formavo answers: is every coach running match days? Is playing time fair club-wide, by the club's own bar (50–75% of team median)? Are players developing in multiple positions? Who's on staff, in what capacity, everywhere? The club pays; coaches and parents ride free.

Against the field

TeamSnap · Spond · Heja

Scheduling-and-messaging apps. Their "live game" is score entry, not a console — no minutes, shot maps, equity, or development logs. Formavo concedes nothing on their turf (schedule, RSVP, chat all exist) while owning a layer they don't have.

Veo & video analysis

Own the high end at a price and workflow youth clubs can't sustain weekly — and no club management substance.

Registration platforms

Own payments and sign-ups, but they're web-era admin tools coaches hate. They never touch the touchline.

The moat, in order: the live console as the data source · equity reporting · staff-only voice notes · the parent-safe permission architecture.
